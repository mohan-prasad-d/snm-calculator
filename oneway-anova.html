Method Overview
Partition of variation
SST = SSB + SSW
SSB = Σ nᵢ(x̄ᵢ − x̄)²  (between groups)
SSW = ΣΣ(xᵢⱼ − x̄ᵢ)²  (within groups)
SST = ΣΣ(xᵢⱼ − x̄)²  (total)
F-test
MSB = SSB / (k − 1)
MSW = SSW / (N − k)
F = MSB / MSW  ∼ F(k−1, N−k)
Hypotheses
H₀: μ₁ = μ₂ = … = μₖ
H₁: at least one population mean is different
ANOVA compares between-group variation with within-group variation.
A large F suggests the group means differ more than chance alone would explain.
Assumptions: independent random samples, approximately normal populations, equal population variances.

Assumptions and notes
  • Observations are independent, and each group is a separate random sample.
  • Classical one-way ANOVA assumes each population is approximately normal.
  • It also assumes equal population variances across groups.
  • Strong skew, outliers or unequal variances call for other methods, such as Welch’s ANOVA or the Kruskal–Wallis test.
  • A significant result says at least one mean differs. It does not identify which groups differ; a post-hoc comparison such as Tukey’s HSD is needed for that.
